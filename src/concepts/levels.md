# Levels

**Personality at Play is where he says the separate traditions can sit in one hierarchy.**

The three he names for that lecture are Jean Piaget, the biologists (neurobiologists, neuropsychologists, and behaviorists), and psychometric approaches to personality.

> "an integrated view of personality theories"
>
> [Personality at Play](lectures/personality-at-play.md).

He describes the method as a hierarchy of conceptualization: put each theory at its level, then unite the levels. He says he thinks that can be brought to a conclusion in the lecture. The claim is the aim he states at the start of that hour, not a proof printed after the fact.

Bridging Psychology's Foundations already names the trait list he is uniting with the rest.

> "the now well-known so-called Big Five models"
>
> [Bridging Psychology’s Foundations](lectures/bridging-psychologys-foundations.md).

In that sentence the five are extraversion, neuroticism, agreeableness, conscientiousness, and openness. He says honesty-humility is sometimes added, and so is the Dark Tetrad. "So-called" is his word. The list is the standard one he is pointing at.
