# State and Process

**Personality, in the opening of Bridging Psychology's Foundations, is what you are and what you are turning into.**

> "people are something, and they're also becoming something."
>
> [Bridging Psychology’s Foundations](lectures/bridging-psychologys-foundations.md).

> "you have to understand it as a state and as a transformative process."
>
> [Bridging Psychology’s Foundations](lectures/bridging-psychologys-foundations.md).

He says the process is the one that matters more, because he takes that process to be consciousness itself.

> "the transformative process is essentially equivalent to consciousness."
>
> [Bridging Psychology’s Foundations](lectures/bridging-psychologys-foundations.md).

He asks why the course is a clinical introduction. His answer is that some personality teachers are social psychologists, and a smaller number are clinical psychologists. The clinical stake is in the same sentence as the theory: a person can get stuck in the process, not only be described as a type.

The same lecture places Jean Piaget with the developmentalists and says personality theorists often leave him out because they do not know how to fit him in. That fit is the job of [Levels](levels.md).
