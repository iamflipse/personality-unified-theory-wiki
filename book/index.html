Personality: A Unified Theory

Personality: A Unified Theory

Eight lectures. About 87488 words. The speaker is not named in the sources.

The argument, as the lectures tell it

  1. A person is a state and a process. People are something, and they are becoming something. Of the two, he treats the becoming as the one that matters, and he equates that process with consciousness. → State and Process
  2. The story form of that becoming is a quest. The quest has two variants, call and conscience. The small person in a protected corner, still subject to larger forces, is his picture of the default human situation. → Call and Conscience
  3. A lot of the person is not available as a sentence. The analytic lectures put Freud first and Jung as the colleague who extended him. Adler is named and then set aside. Dreams are called the royal road. Fiction is how a pattern of action that you cannot yet say becomes sayable. → The Unconscious · The Shadow
  4. The person and the group are one problem. The Rogers lecture says you can make the case that a harmony has to exist between the individual and the social structure, and that this changes what mental health means. → Dialogue
  5. The theories sit at different heights of the same picture. Piaget, the biologists and behaviorists, and the trait measures are brought in as levels, not as rival religions. Positive emotion, in the trait lecture, is the sign that you are moving toward an aim, tied to dopamine. The hypothalamus is where he puts the aim and the frame. → Levels · Aim and Emotion

What these pages do not do

  • They do not supply the speaker's name. It is not in the eight sources.
  • They do not turn a story he uses, such as Bilbo or Frodo, into a finding about those books. The story is his example of call and conscience.
  • They do not treat the Big Five list as his invention. He calls it the now well-known so-called Big Five.
